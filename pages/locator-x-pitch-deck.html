01 / 03 Thesis Locator.X — AGI Future Foundation

Every property score on the market is a guess wearing a number.

Scoring products publish a single confident figure — 82, 94, “A-grade” — computed across county records of wildly unequal quality. A parcel from a county that publishes a recorded use, a year built and a disclosed sale price gets a score that looks exactly like a parcel from a county that publishes a coordinate, an assessed value and nothing else.

Locator.X grades the paperwork before it scores the asset. Every property carries an evidence grade. Every screen carries the count of tests it could not run. Every forecast carries the band its own backtest measured. We ship ~30 county and parish sources and we publish, source by source, exactly what each one refuses to tell us.

The product is not a better number. It is a number an institution can argue with — and that is the part nobody else is selling.

Two ceilings override the arithmetic. A record selected by zoning rather than use caps at 55 — a permission is not evidence a building stands there. A record the source leaves unclassified caps at 34. A grade is a statement about the paperwork, never about the asset.

It discriminates — proof, not impression
Phoenix–Mesa–Chandler, AZ100% A/B
Savannah, GA (Chatham)0% A/B

Chatham publishes zoning and no use class at all, so the 55 ceiling caps every record at C. Not a bug — the intended behaviour and the correct verdict. Competing products would have scored those parcels anyway.

A county that publishes no year built has not made a building old and has not made it new. Folding unknown into fail rejects properties for a gap in a county schema; folding it into meets is worse. Scores compute on what is known, and the count of unanswerable tests travels with every score.
