01 / 03Where the demand actually is Locator.X — Louisiana developer brief

Half the demand Louisiana is priced on has already been cancelled.

Three Louisiana industrial announcements died inside eighteen months — Air Products at Burnside ($4.5bn, cancelled 30 June 2026 against a pre-tax charge of up to $2.9bn), SunGas Renewables at Pineville ($1.8bn, development ceased 12 June 2026), and International Paper at Campti (mill closed April 2025, ~450 jobs). A fourth, Project Cypress in Shreveport, sits on a DOE termination list.

They are still in the feeds. Before we guarded for it, 694 parcels within 25 km of Burnside were advertising a live $4.5bn announcement. Locator.X carries cancelled projects at zero weight, names the cancellation in the property drawer, and re-checks the live project list at render time rather than trusting what was baked in at build.

Two more were corrected rather than carried: Hyundai Steel at Donaldsonville moved from “under construction” to site preparation, and Venture Global at Plaquemines had its $18bn capital figure removed — no company release, no LED page and no FID ever stated it.

A zoning-only record caps at 55 — a district permitting apartments is not evidence a building stands there. An unclassified record caps at 34. That ceiling is why Lafayette can never grade A on the public record, whatever else it publishes.

Distress — where a real feed exists

Of eleven covered counties and parishes, only three publish a genuine jurisdiction-specific distress feed — and East Baton Rouge is one of them. Orleans adds live sheriff and code-enforcement signals. Federal REO from HUD FHA contributes 149 verified cases across the fleet, address and case number only, zero owner PII.

We also corrected our own research module: the claim that counties expose free bulk notice-of-default data was wrong, and is now stated plainly as wrong. No such free feed exists anywhere we verified.
