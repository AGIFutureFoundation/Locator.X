Jobs to housing

Announced direct jobs divided by housing units permitted in the trailing twelve months — a crude measure of how much new demand is arriving per unit of new supply, computed only where both halves are published figures with sources.

What this ratio is, precisely. An announced job is not a filled job — announcements are downsized, delayed and cancelled, and the project record says so on every row. A permit is not a delivered unit, and a metro-wide permit count says nothing about where in the metro the units land. Household formation per job is never 1.0 — it varies by wage level, by how many hires relocate, and by how many already live in the metro. So read the ratio as a way to rank these areas against each other on the same published basis, and never as a number of households. An area missing either half shows "not drawn" rather than a number invented to fill the cell.

Jobs are company and state announcements (the per-project record with sources: Louisiana · California); permits are trailing-twelve-month sums of Census Building Permits Survey monthly observations; population is the most recent published metro estimate. As-of dates shown per cell.

Every caveat carried on the corridor record (37)
  • Every capital and jobs figure in this file was taken from a source URL that was actually fetched in this session. Where a source stated no number, the field is null rather than estimated. Nothing was inferred, annualised or back-calculated except the permit figures, which are explicit twelve-month sums of monthly Census BPS observations listed on the cited FRED data page.
  • Announced jobs are not realised jobs. Intel Ohio has slipped at least five years (production 2030-31 vs an original end-2025), Micron's Clay fab broke ground only in January 2026 for 2030 first output, and LG Energy Solution has reportedly paused part of its Queen Creek build. Phoenix, Savannah and Kansas City are the only metros here whose anchor plants are already producing.
  • TSMC job accounting: the 12,000 direct-jobs figure corresponds to the $165B / six-fab programme announced March 2025 and is sourced to TSMC Arizona president Rose Castanares via TrendForce (https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/05/12/news-tsmcs-165b-arizona-investment-to-create-12000-jobs-once-all-facilities-are-operational/), a secondary source. The July 2026 $100B expansion to $265B and ten fabs published NO incremental jobs number, so Phoenix's 17,000 total is conservative and will understate the eventual figure. TSMC also cites 40,000 construction jobs over four years, excluded from the direct total.
  • TEXAS IS A NON-DISCLOSURE STATE. No sale price appears in the Travis, Williamson or Brazos appraisal data and none is legally obtainable from the CADs. Appraised and market values remain public and are present in the Williamson and Travis endpoints. Any Texas comp model must run on appraisal values rather than transactions. By contrast Arizona, Ohio, Nevada, New York, Virginia, Tennessee and Georgia all expose sale prices in the endpoints cited here.
  • Metros dropped on parcel-data grounds: KANSAS CITY / JOHNSON COUNTY KS publishes no free parcel data at all - its open-data DCAT catalogue was enumerated and contains 40 datasets with zero parcel or land-record entries; AIMS licenses parcel data commercially. CHARLESTON / BERKELEY COUNTY SC exposes parcel geometry and a TMS number only - nine fields, no address, use code, value, year built, square footage or units. HUNTSVILLE / MADISON COUNTY AL has no confirmed county parcel API; the two candidate services tested were a third-party compilation and a mis-identified Madison County OHIO layer respectively.
  • Partial parcel coverage inside otherwise-recommended metros: Savannah's Bryan County (both Hyundai plants) has no working machine-readable parcel service - its ArcGIS host returns a server error and the public route is the qPublic HTML viewer; Reno's Storey County (the Tesla/TRIC site) publishes only PDF parcel maps, though it contains almost no housing; Phoenix's Pinal County (the LG site) distributes assessor data by email request only.
  • Chatham County (Savannah) and Travis County (Austin) both lack building square footage; Chatham, Travis, Brazos and Montgomery TN all lack any unit count. Only WASHOE County NV (UNITS) and HENRICO County VA (NUMBER_UNITS_APARTMENT) carry a true unit count. Franklin County OH (NOCARDS) and Licking County OH (EpinCount) carry CAMA-card counts that approximate it.
  • Coordinates: actual approximate site locations are given for TSMC north Phoenix, Intel Ocotillo, Intel New Albany, Micron Clay, Samsung Taylor, Tesla Giga Texas, Tesla Giga Nevada, the Hyundai Bryan County megasite, Anduril at Rickenbacker, LEGO at Meadowville and Panasonic De Soto. All others are city or town centres. Each project's status string states which.
  • SpaceX 'Terafab' near Bryan-College Station is the least settled entry: the county is contested between sources (Brazos per the August 2026 chamber briefing, Grimes per earlier 2026 reporting), the $16.8B is phase-one only against much larger headline figures circulating in the press, and construction is phased over nine to ten years. Treat it as speculative.
  • The LG Energy Solution / Honda plant at Jeffersonville is listed under Columbus but EXCLUDED from Columbus totals because Fayette County is not in the Columbus MSA. Including it would add $4.4B and 2,200 jobs.
  • Permit data limitations: FRED publishes no metro-level 5+unit multifamily series for most of these metros, so the permit figures given are ALL structure types combined and cannot be split. Charleston is an exception (CHAR745BPPRIV minus CHAR745BP1FH gives a multifamily proxy). The Census annual metro permit files at www2.census.gov are blocked to automated fetching from this environment, so Savannah, Syracuse, Richmond, Huntsville, Clarksville and College Station permits are left null rather than guessed - retrieve them manually from https://www.census.gov/construction/bps/msaannual.html.
  • Population figures are Census Bureau Vintage 2025 metropolitan population estimates (July 1, 2025) accessed via FRED, which redistributes them. Clarksville TN-KY MSA population was not retrieved.
  • All ArcGIS endpoints were verified by fetching their ?f=json layer metadata and reading back the real field list, maxRecordCount and layer name - they returned data without a token. Field CONTENT (population, null rates, currency of the roll) was not sampled; verify with a small query before building against any of them. Endpoints confirmed DEAD or wrong during this pass and deliberately excluded: apps.lickingcounty.gov/.../Auditor/ParcelsFS/FeatureServer, bryangis.bryan-county.org/.../Parcels/MapServer, services.arcgis.com/g1fRTDLeMgspWrYp/.../PFZ_BCADParcels/FeatureServer/0.
  • Larger projects deliberately out of scope or not investigated, any of which could outrank entries here: Texas Instruments Sherman TX (~$30B, Dallas metro adjacent), Amazon New Carlisle IN (~$11B, South Bend), Microsoft Mount Pleasant WI (Racine/Milwaukee), the Stargate/OpenAI Abilene TX campus, xAI Colossus Memphis, and Micron's Boise ID expansion. Louisiana projects (notably Meta Richland Parish) were excluded per instruction, as were all California metros.
  • Every capital and jobs figure in this file was taken from a source URL actually fetched in this session. Where a source published no number the field is null rather than estimated - Microsoft's $13.3B Mount Pleasant expansion has no jobs figure, and permits are null for Albany and South Bend because FRED publishes no metro-level BPS series for either. The only arithmetic performed is the twelve-month permit sums, and the individual monthly observations are written out inside each permits.note so they can be checked against the cited FRED data page.
  • ANNOUNCED JOBS ARE NOT REALISED JOBS, and three of these five have already moved. JetZero broke ground in June 2026 but has cut its 2027 hiring to ZERO and pushed its full 14,500 target to 2037, blaming delayed North Carolina state budget funding; its 14,560 figure is a 37-year incentive-agreement ceiling, so Greensboro's 21,410 total is the most front-loaded-looking and least front-loaded-in-reality number in the file. General Motors EXITED the New Carlisle battery joint venture in August 2026, selling its stake to Samsung SDI, so the 1,700-job commitment there was made by a partnership that no longer exists. Microsoft's $13.3B Mount Pleasant expansion is approved site plans, not committed capital. By contrast Toyota's Randolph County plant (5,100 jobs) and Microsoft's first Mount Pleasant datacenter are ALREADY OPERATING, and the Albany NanoTech EUV Accelerator opened in July 2025.
  • DOUBLE-COUNTING RISK IN SOUTH BEND. Amazon's $11B St. Joseph County campus (April 2024) and its $15B 'northern Indiana' announcement (November 2025) are counted as separate rows totalling $26B. Amazon describes the second as an expansion beyond the first and does not enumerate counties, so an unknown share may fall outside St. Joseph County or restate capacity already counted. Local reporting in July 2026 separately put realised St. Joseph County investment above $13.8B. Treat $29.5B as an upper bound and $13.8B-plus as the verified floor.
  • USE-CODE DESCRIPTION - THE DECISIVE TEST, RESULTS IN PLAIN TERMS. THREE PASS OUTRIGHT: Guilford NC (LAND_CLASS returns literal 'RESIDENTIAL', 'CONDO', 'ASSIST LIV/SKILLCARE', 'GOV OWNED'), St. Joseph IN (PROPTYPE returns '1 Family Dwell - Platted Lot', 'Foundries & Heavy Manufacturing', 'Vacant Land'), and New York State (USED_AS_DESC and BLDG_STYLE_DESC are populated text, and PROP_CLASS has a published ORPTS manual). ONE PASSES ONLY ON A PUBLISHED MANUAL: Wisconsin's PROPCLASS is bare numerals defined in Wis. Stat. 70.32(2)(a) and the Wisconsin Property Assessment Manual, and it returns MULTI-VALUED strings such as '4,5' and '1,4' that must be split before use. ONE IS UNRESOLVED: Marion County IN declares PROPERTY_SUB_CLASS_DESCRIPTION in its layer metadata but four attempts to read actual values failed on read timeouts, so it is asserted by schema and not by observation.
  • SALE PRICE BY STATE, for comp modelling. NORTH CAROLINA and INDIANA are disclosure states and both endpoints carry it - Guilford has two independent fields (PKG_SALE_PRICE and LAND_SALE_PRICE, with SALE_PRICE_SOURCE and REVENUE_STAMPS), St. Joseph has SALESPRICE and SALESDATE though typed as strings. NEW YORK's statewide layer carries FULL_MARKET_VAL, TOTAL_AV and LAND_AV but NO transaction price in this layer. WISCONSIN's statewide layer carries ESTFMKVALUE, CNTASSDVALUE, LNDVALUE and IMPVALUE but NO sale price. INDIANAPOLIS/Marion carries assessed values only. TEXAS and IDAHO, both non-disclosure, were rejected partly on this.
  • UNIT COUNTS. Only ONE of the five endpoints exposes a multifamily unit count directly: Guilford County's TOTAL_UNITS, alongside APT_SC_SQRFT for apartment and shopping-centre floor area. That puts Guilford in the same tier as Henrico VA and makes Greensboro the only metro in this batch where multifamily inventory can be measured rather than inferred. Everywhere else unit counts must be inferred from use class plus floor area, and Wisconsin and Marion County IN have no floor area for residential at all.
  • GEOGRAPHIC ASYMMETRY REPEATS THE PHOENIX PATTERN in two of the five. In INDIANAPOLIS both Lilly projects are in Boone County, which publishes no parcel API of any kind, while the confirmed endpoint covers Marion County only. In GREENSBORO the Toyota plant is in Randolph County, whose endpoint has values but no use code, no year built, no floor area and no units, while the rich endpoint is Guilford. In MILWAUKEE the statewide layer avoids this problem entirely by covering Racine and Ozaukee uniformly, which is the main thing it has going for it.
  • A NAMING TRAP WORTH RECORDING: searching for 'Boone County GIS' returns secure.boonecountygis.com, which is BOONE COUNTY, KENTUCKY, not the Boone County, Indiana of the Lilly LEAP district. The Kentucky service is genuinely excellent (108 fields, three generations of sale price, assessed value, zoning, assessment type) and returns data without a token, but it is the wrong state.
  • WEBFETCH BEHAVIOUR NOTE, in case these endpoints are re-tested: ArcGIS /query URLs carrying long comma-separated outFields lists were repeatedly refused with HTTP 403 by the fetch proxy, while the same query with one or two outFields succeeded. Where a field-value sample is cited in this file it was obtained with a short outFields list. Counts were obtained with returnCountOnly=true.
  • Every parcel endpoint reported as available in this file was queried in this session and returned rows. Row counts came from where=1=1&returnCountOnly=true against the exact endpoint URL given. Field lists came from the layer's own f=pjson metadata. Distinct use/zoning values came from returnDistinctValues queries. No endpoint is reported that I did not successfully query - the Lafayette Parish Assessor's own webgis.lafayetteassessor.com service is listed in the rejected array for exactly that reason, even though the parish itself is recommended via a different, working endpoint.
  • Outbound HTTPS in this session was restricted to a fetch-and-summarise proxy, so responses were read as summarised text rather than raw JSON. Counts, field names and distinct values were requested verbatim, but a very long distinct-value list can be truncated by that layer; treat the distinct-value lists as representative rather than exhaustive.

  • Two of the five recommended parcel endpoints are NOT published by the parish assessor: the Lafayette layer is hosted by Es2 (es2-inc), the assessor's GIS vendor, and the Caddo layer is a Regrid/Loveland extract republished by a private economic-development ArcGIS Online account.   • The two-letter Louisiana assessor class codes (RI, RV, CM, CI, AI, EX, RM ...) appear in BOTH the Lincoln Parish assessor layer and the Caddo Regrid extract, so they are a statewide convention rather than a local one. I could not find a published key for them anywhere and have not guessed at their meanings. If you can obtain that key from the Louisiana Tax Commission or an assessor's office, Lincoln Parish immediately becomes a much stronger candidate and Caddo's usecode field becomes usable alongside its zoning.
  • Louisiana is NOT a mandatory sale-price disclosure state in the way Arizona is, but sale price is nevertheless populated in the Lafayette (saleprice/saledate), Caddo (saleprice/saledate) and Tangipahoa (purchase_price/transfer_date, with a Verified flag) layers.   • Confirmed but rejected as a data source: https://gissrv1.romlc.net/arcgis/rest/services/WebMaps/LA_Assessors/MapServer, which is indexed on ArcGIS Online as the parcel source for Caddo, Bossier, Tangipahoa, Natchitoches and other parishes, returns error 499 'Token Required' and is unusable without credentials.
  • Campus proximity is scored from a published-enrollment table that now carries 118 institutions across every corridor in this catalogue — 1.2 million students — each with the term its figure refers to and a source URL. Community colleges are weighted down, health-science campuses partially. Where a figure covers more than one campus or includes online enrolment, the row says so; a wrong denominator would silently corrupt a student-housing score. One institution, Purdue University in Indianapolis, carries a null enrolment because no campus-level figure has been published since the 2024 IUPUI split.
  • Four project figures in this set are known not to match their announcements and are recorded that way: Apple's $1bn Research Triangle campus, promised in 2021 with 3,000 jobs, has never broken ground and North Carolina reset its milestones in November 2025 to require only 126 people by end-2027; Maxeon's $1bn Albuquerque plant was CANCELLED, its lease abandoned in February 2026; Intel promised 700 jobs at Rio Rancho and described delivery as 'hundreds'; and Novo Nordisk's Clayton expansion was followed by layoffs at that same plant. Counting Apple's 3,000 would move Raleigh from fourth to first on the jobs-per-permit ranking, and counting Maxeon would move Albuquerque from fourth to second. Neither is counted.

  • Four Louisiana college markets (Monroe, Thibodaux, Natchitoches, Alexandria) were researched in September 2026 and are carried as STUDIED, not live: they appear in the corridor tables with their verified enrolment, capital and permit figures, but they hold no parcels on the map.
