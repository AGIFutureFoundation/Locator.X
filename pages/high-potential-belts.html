The High-Potential Belts

Where rent is outrunning price: 464 US submarkets ranked on what they pay now rather than what they have appreciated — sixty-one months of value and rent series, scored in the open, leading with the 12 states this covers and the 39 it does not.

Coverage, before anything derived from it

This is not a map of the United States. The index bundle carries both a value and a rent series for ZIPs in twelve states; the other thirty-nine are absent — not scored low, not measured. Of 737 ZIPs carrying both series, 464 cleared the data floor (48+ monthly points); 273 were dropped for a series too thin to trust.

Covered (12): AZ CA GA IN LA NC NM NV NY OH UT WI

Absent entirely (39): AK AL AR CO CT DC DE FL HI IA ID IL KS KY MA MD ME MI MN MO MS MT ND NE NH NJ OK OR PA RI SC SD TN TX VA VT WA WV WY

How the score is built, and how to disagree with it

Each component is percentile-ranked across the 464 candidates, then weighted over only the components a submarket can actually answer — a missing component is counted and shown, never imputed as average. Cash flow before capital gains: price appreciation alone never drives a rank. Ranked at ZIP grain because that is what the data supports — only 31 metros carry both series, so a hundred metros does not exist to be listed.
