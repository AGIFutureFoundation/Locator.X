Someone else reads the sentence.

The Academy can tell you that a figure in your thesis appears nowhere in the record, and that you leaned on a gate the record could not answer. It cannot tell you whether the thesis is any good. That has always needed a second person, and this is where they are.

Three things happen here that a single-player course cannot do: a deal thesis gets read by someone who did not write it, the response follows the same decision-memo discipline the course teaches rather than dissolving into encouragement, and individual records pool into the one figure a single browser can never produce — which drills actually fail, across people.

Theses under review

Post the sentence you would defend to a partner, with what the Academy already found in it. The two counts are not a score — they are the two things a machine can check, so that the people reading can spend their attention on the part it cannot.

The pooled record

Each edition of Locator.X keeps your progress in your own browser and says plainly that it cannot tell you anything about anyone else — there is no cross-viewer store in a file that runs with the network off. This is that store. Paste the JSON your copy exports and it joins the pool. The floor travels with it: a rate is printed only where enough answers back it, and the count of records is stated before anything derived from them.
