# Northeast — CT · ME · MA · NH · NJ · NY · PA · RI · VT

State-by-state localization of the [Locator.X process](README.md). The region's common
threads: judicial or court-adjacent foreclosure in most states (slow distress pipelines),
attorney-heavy closings, town/municipal record-keeping in New England (the *town*, not the
county, holds the record), and the Vision Government Solutions / AxisGIS / CAI-run assessor
portals across New England. Verify everything against the current source; see the
[disclaimer](README.md).

---

## Connecticut

**Run the record here.** No county government — 169 towns hold the record. Assessor cards are
mostly on Vision Government Solutions ("VGSI") portals; land records at each town clerk, with
many towns on shared e-recording indexes. Statewide parcels via CT DEEP / UConn CT ECO parcel
layer.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure only, including **strict foreclosure** (title passes
without sale when equity is thin) — pipeline is slow and lawyer-driven. Tax collectors run
redeemable tax sales (six-month redemption typical). Attorney-closing state. Mandatory
residential property condition report, with a credit in lieu.

**Programs & money.** CHFA (Connecticut Housing Finance Authority): first-time buyer loans,
down-payment assistance, multifamily and LIHTC allocation. State historic rehabilitation tax
credit stacks on the federal credit in the mill towns. Municipal tax-abatement deals are
negotiated town by town.

**Locator.X angle.** Ownership and value gates answer well (VGSI cards are rich: sketches,
sales history). Distress gate is slow-moving by design — the judicial calendar *is* the
pipeline. Mill-conversion stock in Hartford/New Haven/Waterbury rings fits the A7 conversion
lens.

## Maine

**Run the record here.** Towns assess; the Maine GeoLibrary publishes a statewide parcel
viewer of varying vintage. Registries of deeds are county-level and largely searchable online.
Unorganized territory is assessed by the state.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure. Municipal tax liens foreclose **automatically** by
statute after the redemption window — towns end up owning real estate and sell it by bid, a
quiet acquisition channel. Transfer tax split buyer/seller. Attorney closings customary.

**Programs & money.** MaineHousing: first-home program, multifamily financing, LIHTC. Strong
historic credit (25% state, refundable in part) drives mill and downtown conversions.
Municipal TIF is used aggressively for development deals.

**Locator.X angle.** Town-by-town record quality varies more than almost anywhere; grade
coverage per town, not per county. Municipally foreclosed property lists are a distress source
with no auction competition dynamics.

## Massachusetts

**Run the record here.** MassGIS publishes one of the best statewide standardized parcel
layers in the country (L3 parcels with assessor extract). Registries of deeds unified at
masslandrecords.com. Registered (Land Court) vs recorded land distinction matters at title.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial power of sale, but with a mandatory Land Court
Servicemembers-Act step that adds months. Municipal tax **takings** are foreclosed in Land
Court; the 2023 *Tyler v. Hennepin* line of cases reshaped surplus-equity practice — verify
current statute. Deed excise on transfers. Attorney-closing state.

**Programs & money.** MassHousing and MassDevelopment (bond financing, brownfields);
Chapter 40B streamlines permits for mixed-income projects over local zoning; MassSave rebates
land in the operating stack. Boston and Gateway Cities carry their own program layers.

**Locator.X angle.** Best-in-region evidence ceiling: MassGIS makes the L gate nearly
statewide-uniform. Zoning (D2/D3 courses) is the binding constraint — entitlement risk, not
record quality, is what the memo must carry.

## New Hampshire

**Run the record here.** Towns assess (many on Vision or Avitar portals); NH GRANIT hosts the
statewide parcel mosaic. County registries of deeds are online.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial power of sale — fastest foreclosure clock in New England.
Municipal tax deeding: the town takes a deed after redemption lapses and may sell. Transfer
tax at a meaningful rate, split buyer/seller. No state income tax on wages shifts the fiscal
mix onto property tax — expense-stack it.

**Programs & money.** New Hampshire Housing (NHHFA): buyer programs, multifamily, LIHTC.
InvestNH and municipal housing-champion grants have funded recent multifamily. High
property-tax towns effectively price-discriminate against thin-margin deals — the tax line is
the feasibility gate.

**Locator.X angle.** T gate prices the fast foreclosure clock; O (outlook) must carry the
property-tax trajectory, which towns reset by revaluation cycles.

## New Jersey

**Run the record here.** County boards of taxation publish MOD-IV assessment records
statewide (searchable via the state's Assessment Records Search); NJGIN publishes statewide
parcels. County clerks/registers hold deeds and mortgages, most with online indexes.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure with one of the longest timelines in the country.
**Tax lien state with 18% statutory interest** and premium-bid municipal auctions — an entire
local industry; post-*Tyler* reforms are live, verify current practice. Attorney closings in
the north; title-company closings common in the south. Realty transfer fee plus the "mansion
tax" tiers.

**Programs & money.** NJHMFA (buyer and multifamily programs); NJEDA's Aspire and Emerge tax
credits fund large redevelopment; PILOT agreements (5- and 30-year) are the standard local
incentive — a negotiated expense line unique to each municipality.

**Locator.X angle.** MOD-IV gives a uniform statewide O gate few states match. The lien
calendar is a data-rich distress signal (municipal lists are public), but redemption behavior
makes it a yield play more than an acquisition channel.

## New York

**Run the record here.** NYC: ACRIS (deeds/mortgages) plus DOF assessment roll and PLUTO —
among the richest open property data anywhere. Outside NYC: county real property tax service
offices, many on SDG Image Mate; statewide GIS program publishes a parcel layer. County
clerks hold instruments.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure, multi-year in downstate courts. Tax enforcement
varies: NYC sells lien pools to a trust; upstate counties run deed foreclosures or lien sales
county-by-county. State transfer tax plus NYC RPTT and the mansion tax. Attorney-closing
custom statewide. Rent regulation (HSTPA 2019) is the underwriting event for multifamily —
regulated rolls price like a different asset class.

**Programs & money.** NYS HCR consolidates the HFA/SONYMA programs; NYC HPD runs its own
universe (J-51 successor, 421-a successor "485-x", Article XI). 485-x and city PILOTs decide
multifamily feasibility in NYC more than cap rates do.

**Locator.X angle.** NYC's record answers all seven gates unusually well — the constraint is
regulatory overlay, not data. Upstate is the inverse: thinner portals, county-variable tax
process. Underwrite the two as different states.

## Pennsylvania

**Run the record here.** County-by-county: assessment offices (many on Tyler/iasWorld or
county-run sites), recorders of deeds with online search in most counties. PASDA aggregates
county parcel layers where counties allow. Philadelphia's OPA/Atlas is fully open.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure. Two-stage county tax sales: **upset sale** (liens
survive) then **judicial sale** (divested) — plus Philadelphia's separate sheriff-sale
system; buy at the wrong stage and you inherit the liens. State transfer tax plus local
(Philadelphia's combined rate is among the highest). Title companies close; attorneys common.
Assessment base years vary by county — appeal math is county-specific.

**Programs & money.** PHFA (Keystone programs, PHARE fund, multifamily/LIHTC); LERTA local
tax abatements; Philadelphia's ten-year abatement (now tapered) still shapes new-build
economics; RACP state grants for larger projects.

**Locator.X angle.** The upset-vs-judicial distinction is the classic certainty-error trap:
the record can say "sold at tax sale" while liens ride through. Gate T must state which sale
stage the title came through, or say unknown.

## Rhode Island

**Run the record here.** Cities/towns assess (Vision portals predominate); land evidence
records are held at the municipal level, not county. RIGIS publishes statewide parcels.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial power of sale. Redeemable tax sales: buyer takes a tax title,
then petitions to foreclose the right of redemption after one year. Attorney closings.
Transfer tax on the deed.

**Programs & money.** RIHousing (buyer, developer and LIHTC programs); Rebuild RI tax credit
for development gaps; Providence historic districts stack state and federal credits.

**Locator.X angle.** Small enough to hold the whole state in one buy box; the tax-title
seasoning period is the number to model on distress entries.

## Vermont

**Run the record here.** Towns keep land records at the town clerk — the most decentralized
recording system in the country; many clerks now on Cott/Kofile indexes. The statewide parcel
program publishes a unified layer; grand lists (assessments) are town-maintained.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure (strict foreclosure available). Municipal tax sales
carry a one-year redemption. Property transfer tax with a land-gains overlay on short-hold
land flips — a state-specific exit-arithmetic line. Attorney closings.

**Programs & money.** VHFA (buyer and multifamily programs, state LIHTC); Vermont downtown
and village-center tax credits; Act 250 is the entitlement regime — a statewide land-use
permit layered over local zoning, and the schedule risk that gates development (D3's course
material, in statute form).

**Locator.X angle.** Act 250 is the O gate's dominant unknown for anything development-shaped;
the land-gains tax must sit in any short-hold model. Record coverage is town-by-town — grade
it that way.
