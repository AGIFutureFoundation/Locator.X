# Midwest — IL · IN · IA · KS · MI · MN · MO · NE · ND · OH · SD · WI

State-by-state localization of the [Locator.X process](README.md). Regional threads: judicial
foreclosure through most of the eastern Midwest (IL, IN, OH, WI) against
foreclosure-by-advertisement in MI/MN; county tax-forfeiture regimes reshaped by
*Tyler v. Hennepin* (2023, a Minnesota case — surplus-equity rules changed region-wide, so
verify every forfeiture state's current statute); and the Beacon/qPublic + DEVNET + Vanguard
portal patterns that make county screening cheap. Verify everything against the current
source; see the [disclaimer](README.md).

---

## Illinois

**Run the record here.** Cook County is its own ecosystem (Assessor, Clerk, Recorder all
online, plus the Cook Central open-data portal); collar and downstate counties largely on
DEVNET wEdge or Beacon. Recorders are county-level; statewide parcels via Illinois
Geospatial Data Clearinghouse.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure, slow in Cook. **Tax lien state with the annual sale
bid-down** (interest bid from 9% downward per period) plus scavenger-sale reform in Cook —
an entire specialist industry; redemption periods run ~2–3 years. Cook's assessment
classification (higher commercial ratios) and appeal culture are an operating discipline.
Transfer taxes at state, county, and (Chicago) municipal tiers — Chicago's is meaningful and
belongs in exit math. Attorney closings in Chicago custom.

**Programs & money.** IHDA (buyer programs, LIHTC + state credit); Cook County landbank and
Chicago's own incentive classes (6b industrial, 7 commercial) cut assessed ratios —
mappable, applied-for subsidies; TIF is everywhere in Chicago development.

**Locator.X angle.** Cook vs downstate is two regimes; the classification system means an
identical building carries different tax physics across a county line. The lien bid-down
sale is yield-first, acquisition-second.

## Indiana

**Run the record here.** DLGF's statewide portals plus Beacon for most counties; recorders
online through Doxpop in many. Statewide parcel layer via IndianaMap — good, free coverage.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure. County **tax lien sales** each fall (commissioners'
certificate sales for leftovers at reduced minimums) with one-year redemption; tax-deed
petitions follow. No transfer tax — cheap exits. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** IHCDA (buyer DPA, LIHTC); Indianapolis' Mile Square/TIF layers;
Regional Cities / READI grants have funded mixed-use in the mid-size metros.

**Locator.X angle.** Commissioners' sales (the second-chance certificates) are where minimums
drop below lien value — the screening edge. Free statewide parcels + no transfer tax make
Indiana one of the cheapest states to run the full seven gates.

## Iowa

**Run the record here.** Beacon (Schneider) covers most counties' assessor data; recorders
online via county sites/Iowa Land Records — a **statewide electronic land-records portal**,
rare and excellent. Statewide parcels published.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure (nonjudicial voluntary alternative exists); no
deficiency in some tracks shortens timelines. Annual June **tax lien sales** (certificate,
~2% monthly interest, 1.75-year redemption before deed). Transfer tax modest. Abstract +
attorney-opinion title custom (like Oklahoma) — title insurance is not the default; Iowa
Title Guaranty covers instead.

**Programs & money.** Iowa Finance Authority (FirstHome, LIHTC); workforce-housing tax
credits with rural set-asides — an unusually usable small-deal development credit.

**Locator.X angle.** Iowa Land Records collapses gate O statewide. The workforce credit
changes small-town feasibility math (D4) more than any comparable state program.

## Kansas

**Run the record here.** County appraisers publish via ORKA/Beacon-style portals; registers
of deeds online in urban counties; state GIS hub publishes parcels for many counties.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure with post-sale redemption (commonly 3–12 months —
it lengthens effective timelines). County **judicial tax foreclosure deed auctions** for
multi-year delinquencies. No transfer tax; mortgage registration tax repealed. Abstract
custom persists in rural counties.

**Programs & money.** KHRC (first-time buyer, LIHTC, moderate-income housing grants for
rural counties — a real gap-filler under 60k-population counties).

**Locator.X angle.** The redemption tail after sheriff's sales is the timing number
distress models miss. Johnson/Sedgwick counties carry most of the state's liquidity; the V3
sample floor binds nearly everywhere else.

## Michigan

**Run the record here.** Local (city/township) assessors under county equalization; BS&A
Online hosts a huge share of assessment/tax records statewide — one vendor pattern covering
most of the state; registers of deeds online per county.

**Process notes.** Foreclosure **by advertisement** (nonjudicial) with a six-month
redemption after sale — the redemption, not the sale, is the timeline. Tax delinquency runs
the **county-treasurer forfeiture/foreclosure** track ending in summer **deed auctions**
(tax-sale.info pattern); *Tyler*-driven surplus-claim reforms are live. Transfer tax state +
county. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** MSHDA (MI Home Loan, LIHTC, mod rehab), county landbanks (Genesee
pioneered the model; Detroit Land Bank is the largest inventory holder in the country) —
side-door acquisition channels with compliance strings; OPRA/NEZ abatements in Detroit
change tax lines dramatically.

**Locator.X angle.** Landbank + auction + abatement is a full acquisition stack unique in
scale to Michigan. Post-auction redemption windows and landbank compliance clocks belong in
gate R timelines.

## Minnesota

**Run the record here.** County assessor/recorder portals (Hennepin/Ramsey strong);
MetroGIS/state Geospatial Commons publish parcels statewide. Torrens (registered) land is
common in Hennepin — check which system a parcel sits in.

**Process notes.** Foreclosure by advertisement with **six-month post-sale redemption**
(owner keeps possession through it). Tax-forfeiture to the **state**, sold at county land
sales — this is the *Tyler v. Hennepin* home state; surplus-equity handling was rewritten,
verify current statute. Deed tax + mortgage registry tax. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** Minnesota Housing (Start Up, LIHTC + state HTC contribution credit);
Twin Cities' 2040-plan legalized missing-middle (duplex/triplex on single-family lots) —
an entitlement tailwind priced into land.

**Locator.X angle.** The redemption-with-possession rule means "bought at sheriff's sale"
delivers cash flow six months late — model it. Torrens vs abstract changes title costs
per parcel.

## Missouri

**Run the record here.** County assessors online (St. Louis City is an independent city —
the Virginia-style join hazard); recorders of deeds per county; state publishes parcels for
many counties.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial trust-deed foreclosure — one of the fastest clocks anywhere
(sale possible in ~30–45 days from first notice). Annual August **tax lien sales** in most
counties (three offerings with shrinking redemption); St. Louis City and Kansas City run
judicial land-tax suits (Land Reutilization Authority inventory in St. Louis). No transfer
tax. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** MHDC (buyer, LIHTC + state matching credit — one of the larger state
credits); Chapter 353/99 abatements and LCRA structures in KC/St. Louis cut taxes on rehab
for a decade-plus.

**Locator.X angle.** The abatement layers are the KC/STL underwriting story — a parcel's
abatement status changes NOI class. The speed of nonjudicial sale makes the pre-foreclosure
window, not the auction, the acquisition moment.

## Nebraska

**Run the record here.** County assessors publish through the state's standardized
valuation lookup plus GIS Workshop/Vanguard portals; registers of deeds online in
Douglas/Lancaster/Sarpy.

**Process notes.** Trust deeds foreclose nonjudicially; mortgages judicially. Annual March
**tax lien sales** (14% statutory, round-robin bidding), three-year redemption before
treasurer's deed or judicial foreclosure of the certificate. Documentary stamp (transfer)
tax present. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** NIFA (first-home loans, LIHTC); TIF is the standard local tool
(Omaha uses it broadly); rural workforce-housing funds.

**Locator.X angle.** Round-robin lien sales remove bid-down competition — yields are
statutory, allocation is luck; treat as yield only. Omaha/Lincoln carry the liquidity;
elsewhere the sample floor binds.

## North Dakota

**Run the record here.** County directors of tax equalization publish parcel data (Vanguard
portals common); recorders online in Cass/Burleigh; the state GIS hub aggregates parcels.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure only (anti-deficiency-flavored, farm-protective
history). Tax delinquency forfeits to the **county** after the statutory period; counties
hold annual **November deed auctions**. No transfer tax. Abstract custom persists.

**Programs & money.** NDHFA (FirstHome, LIHTC); Renaissance Zone programs (Fargo's is the
model) give multi-year property and income tax exemptions on downtown rehab — one of the
cleanest small-city incentive designs anywhere.

**Locator.X angle.** Renaissance-zone math can flip a marginal D4 feasibility; the state is
small enough that county auction calendars can be inventoried completely.

## Ohio

**Run the record here.** County auditors publish uniformly strong parcel sites (a
statutory duty — Franklin, Cuyahoga, Hamilton are excellent, with bulk downloads);
recorders online statewide.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure through sheriff's sales (now often on the statewide
Realauction platform). Tax delinquency is **both**: negotiated **lien-certificate sales**
to institutional buyers in large counties, and judicial **tax foreclosure deed sales**;
expedited board-of-revision foreclosure moves vacant parcels to landbanks. County landbanks
(Cuyahoga model) are region-defining. Transfer tax (conveyance fee) per county. Title
agencies close.

**Programs & money.** OHFA (buyer, LIHTC + new state LIHTC), historic credit;
CRA property-tax abatements (residential rehab/new-build) city by city; JobsOhio-adjacent
site programs. Columbus/Cincinnati/Cleveland run three distinct market clocks.

**Locator.X angle.** Auditor bulk data makes Ohio a first-class screening state — all seven
gates run cheaply. Know which delinquency track a parcel is on (cert vs judicial vs
landbank) before calling it pipeline.

## South Dakota

**Run the record here.** County directors of equalization publish through Vanguard/GIS
Workshop portals; registers of deeds online in Minnehaha/Pennington; state parcel coverage
partial.

**Process notes.** Both foreclosure tracks; judicial common, with post-sale redemption
(shortened under voluntary provisions). Counties hold tax lien certificates (public sale of
certificates largely discontinued — counties take deed after the redemption period and sell
surplus at auction). Transfer fee modest. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** SDHDA (first-time buyer, LIHTC, Governor's House affordable
kit-home program — an actual state-built housing product); no state income tax.

**Locator.X angle.** Thin markets outside Sioux Falls/Rapid City keep the V3 floor binding;
county surplus-deed sales are irregular but uncontested when they happen.

## Wisconsin

**Run the record here.** Statewide parcel map (V9+ program) is genuinely statewide;
county/municipal assessors publish rolls; registers of deeds online. Milwaukee and Dane
publish deep open data.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure with election-of-remedy redemption trade-offs
(shorter timeline if deficiency waived). Tax delinquency: counties take deed by **in rem
foreclosure** and sell surplus (Milwaukee City ran its own in rem program and resale
inventory). Transfer fee on deeds. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** WHEDA (buyer loans, LIHTC + state credit); Milwaukee's tax-foreclosed
home programs and TIF-heavy development practice; historic credit meaningful.

**Locator.X angle.** The statewide parcel program plus in-rem resale lists make Wisconsin's
distress and screening layers cleaner than its neighbors'; the judicial redemption
trade-off is the lender-side timing number for gate T.
