# Southeast — DE · DC · FL · GA · MD · NC · SC · VA · WV

State-by-state localization of the [Locator.X process](README.md). Regional threads:
nonjudicial speed in GA/VA/NC/WV against judicial FL/DE/SC; attorney-closing states (GA, SC,
NC, WV, DE) beside title-company markets; the qPublic/Beacon (Schneider Geospatial) assessor
portal pattern across GA, FL panhandle and the Carolinas; and coastal insurance as a
first-class expense line. Verify everything against the current source; see the
[disclaimer](README.md).

---

## Delaware

**Run the record here.** Three counties. New Castle, Kent and Sussex each publish parcel
search and GIS (FirstMap is the statewide layer); recorders of deeds are online. Small enough
to inventory completely.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure by scire facias. Delinquent taxes collected through
**monition sales** (county sheriff deed sales). Combined state + county + (sometimes)
municipal transfer tax runs to roughly 4% — among the highest in the country, and it belongs
in every exit model. Attorney-closing state.

**Programs & money.** DSHA (Delaware State Housing Authority): buyer assistance, LIHTC,
Downtown Development Districts rebate (a meaningful capex rebate in designated districts —
Wilmington, Dover, Seneca towns).

**Locator.X angle.** The transfer-tax drag is the state's signature number: gate O must carry
it on both entry and exit. DDD district boundaries are a mappable subsidy layer.

## District of Columbia

**Run the record here.** A city-state: OTR's real property database, the Recorder of Deeds,
and Open Data DC (parcels, permits, zoning — deep and free).

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial foreclosure permitted but mediation rules slow it; much
multifamily distress resolves through receivership. Annual **tax lien sale** by OTR. TOPA —
the Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act — is the underwriting event for any occupied
multifamily: tenant rights add months and real optionality against you. Transfer + recordation
taxes on both sides.

**Programs & money.** DCHFA (bonds, LIHTC), DHCD (HPTF — one of the best-funded local housing
trust funds per capita), tax abatements by council act; Opportunity Zones overlay east of the
river.

**Locator.X angle.** Open Data DC pushes the evidence ceiling high; TOPA timelines are the O
gate's dominant term. Model DC as its own market — it behaves like neither Maryland nor
Virginia.

## Florida

**Run the record here.** County property appraisers are constitutionally separate offices
with uniformly strong websites; clerks of court run recording and foreclosure dockets online.
Florida DOR publishes statewide assessment rolls (NAL/SDF files) — a bulk-data gift. FGDL
aggregates GIS.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure, high-volume and comparatively fast for a judicial
state. Tax system is the classic **hybrid**: lien certificates auctioned online each June
(bid-down interest from 18%), then after two years the certificate holder can force a **tax
deed** application sale. Homestead assessment caps (Save Our Homes) make the tax bill jump on
purchase — recalculate, never inherit, the tax line. Doc stamps on deeds and notes. Title
companies close; attorneys in some markets.

**Programs & money.** Florida Housing (SAIL, LIHTC, Hometown Heroes buyer program); Live
Local Act preemptions and property-tax exemptions for qualifying affordable multifamily —
a major 2023+ underwriting change; PACE is active statewide. County CRAs (community
redevelopment agencies) run TIF districts.

**Locator.X angle.** Bulk DOR rolls make Florida the easiest large state to screen wholesale
— the L and A gates run statewide. The insurance line is the survival number: wind/flood
premiums have repriced whole submarkets; DSCR at quoted insurance, not trailing.

## Georgia

**Run the record here.** County tax assessors mostly on qPublic/Beacon; clerks' deed records
unified statewide on the GSCCCA index — one login for every county's instruments, unusual and
valuable.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial foreclosure under **security deeds** (title-theory), first
Tuesday of the month on the courthouse steps — among the fastest clocks anywhere. Tax sales
produce **redeemable deeds**: 20% premium if redeemed within a year, then barment perfects
title. Attorney-closing state (closing attorney represents the lender). Transfer tax modest.

**Programs & money.** DCA/Georgia Dream (buyer), GHFA (LIHTC — state credit pairs with
federal), Invest Georgia and local development authorities issue bond-lease abatements
("bonds for title") that cut property tax on big projects — county-negotiated.

**Locator.X angle.** GSCCCA collapses the O gate's cost statewide. The first-Tuesday cadence
gives the distress pipeline a metronome; the redeemable-deed premium is a priced 20%/1-year
option — model it exactly, not vaguely.

## Maryland

**Run the record here.** SDAT publishes statewide assessments and ownership (Real Property
Data Search) — one portal for every county; MDLandRec.net serves land records statewide free.
Baltimore City is its own recording jurisdiction.

**Process notes.** Foreclosure is quasi-judicial (order to docket, court-ratified sale).
County **tax lien sales** annually, redemption via circuit-court foreclosure of the right of
redemption; Baltimore City's sale carries its own reform history — verify current rules.
State + county transfer/recordation taxes vary meaningfully by county. Title companies close.

**Programs & money.** Maryland DHCD: Maryland Mortgage Program, Rental Housing Works, state
LIHTC ("Maryland Affordable Housing Trust"), historic credit; Baltimore's Vacants to Value
and receivership programs are an acquisition channel unique to the city.

**Locator.X angle.** SDAT + MDLandRec give a full statewide O gate for free. County
transfer-tax deltas are large enough to move the exit math — carry them per county, not as a
state constant.

## North Carolina

**Run the record here.** County assessor/GIS portals are strong statewide (NC OneMap
aggregates parcels); registers of deeds are online in nearly every county. Revaluation
cycles (4–8 years) drive tax-bill step-changes.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial power of sale with a clerk-of-court hearing — fast but with a
paper trail. Tax foreclosures are **deed** sales (mortgage-style or in-rem), with a 10-day
upset-bid window that reopens on every raise — auctions can run for months in slow motion.
Attorney-closing state. Modest excise (transfer) tax.

**Programs & money.** NCHFA (NC Home Advantage, LIHTC + state workforce housing loan
program); tiered county incentive grants (JDIG/OneNC at the state level for
employment-linked projects). Charlotte/Raleigh corridor growth is the market story.

**Locator.X angle.** The upset-bid mechanism means "won at auction" is not a fact until the
window closes — a literal certainty-error trap for gate R. Revaluation years are mappable
tax-shock events; screen for them.

## South Carolina

**Run the record here.** County assessors on qPublic/Beacon or county-run sites; registers of
deeds online in the populous counties. The 4% (owner-occupied) vs 6% (investor) assessment
ratio is the single most consequential record field.

**Process notes.** Judicial foreclosure. Annual county **tax sales**: lien-style receipt,
one-year redemption at escalating interest, deed if unredeemed. The 6% ratio plus loss of
owner-occupant caps means an investor's tax bill can be a multiple of the seller's — the
classic SC underwriting mistake. Attorney-closing state (supreme-court mandated). Deed
recording fee acts as the transfer tax.

**Programs & money.** SC Housing (buyer programs, LIHTC + state credit), abandoned-buildings
and textile-mill revitalization credits (powerful for conversions), county fee-in-lieu (FILOT)
agreements for larger projects.

**Locator.X angle.** Never carry the seller's tax line into the pro forma — recompute at 6%
with millage. The mill-credit stack pairs directly with the A7 conversion lens.

## Virginia

**Run the record here.** Independent cities sit outside counties (Richmond ≠ Richmond
County affects every join — a V2-course example in the wild). Local commissioners of revenue
/ assessors publish records; circuit court clerks hold deeds with a mix of subscription and
free indexes. VGIN publishes a statewide parcel layer.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial foreclosure, fast. Delinquent taxes collected through
**judicial sale** (bill in equity) or nonjudicial auction for low-value parcels — county
attorneys publish lists. Recordation + grantor taxes. Title companies and attorneys both
close. Disclosure regime is caveat-emptor-leaning ("buyer beware" acknowledgment form).

**Programs & money.** Virginia Housing (formerly VHDA — buyer loans with its own MI,
multifamily, LIHTC) plus DHCD (Vibrant Community Initiative, historic credit among the
country's strongest at 25% state).

**Locator.X angle.** The independent-city geography is the join hazard; key on FIPS, not on
name. The state historic credit changes conversion residuals materially — run D4 feasibility
with and without it.

## West Virginia

**Run the record here.** County assessors publish through the state's integrated
property-tax system (MapWV/property viewer gives statewide parcels); county clerks hold
deeds, some online. Flat, cheap record access relative to neighbors.

**Process notes.** Nonjudicial trust-deed foreclosure. Delinquent land process runs through
the **State Auditor**: certification, lien-style sale, deed after redemption lapses (reformed
2022 — verify current mechanics). Attorney closings customary. Transfer tax present, low.

**Programs & money.** WVHDF (buyer and rental programs, LIHTC); BUILD WV Act incentives for
housing development in designated districts; abandoned/dilapidated-property programs at
municipal level.

**Locator.X angle.** Low absolute prices mean the sample-floor rule (V3) binds constantly —
county medians sit on tiny counts; hold the evidence grade honest. The Auditor's delinquent
lists are a centralized statewide distress feed most states lack.
