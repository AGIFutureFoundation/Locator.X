# Case: the duration mismatch — WeWork's leases, 2019–2024

*A candidate for the Academy's record layer (roadmap v1.3), drafted docs-side under the
record layer's own discipline: a case study from **published filings**, each claim marked
**[documented]** (a filing, docket or court record), **[reported]** (press or company
announcement), or **[disputed]**. Judge the decision, not the outcome. Porting this into
the in-app record track changes lesson counts and follows the module supply workflow

## The claims

| # | Claim | Mark |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | WeWork's IPO prospectus (Form S-1, published 2019-08-14) disclosed **$47.2 billion of future undiscounted minimum lease payment obligations** against **$4.0 billion of committed revenue backlog** | **[documented]** — S-1; widely quoted contemporaneously |
| 2 | The S-1 described US leases with an **average initial term of ~15 years**, while member agreements were largely short-term/month-to-month | **[documented]** — S-1 (verify verbatim against the filing before in-app use) |
| 3 | WeWork Inc. and **516 affiliated debtors** filed chapter 11 on **2023-11-06** in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court, **District of New Jersey** (lead case 23-19865) | **[documented]** — court docket |
| 4 | Q3 2023 reporting put lease obligations around **$13 billion**, most due 2028 or later | **[reported]** — press summarizing the company's earnings report |
| 5 | First-day papers cited lease-portfolio rationalization, pandemic-era return-to-office shortfalls and inflation as principal causes | **[documented]** — first-day declaration, as summarized in contemporaneous counsel alerts |
| 6 | The plan equitized roughly **$4 billion of secured debt**; senior creditors became the owners and pre-petition equity was extinguished | **[documented]** — plan of reorganization; corroborated by press |
| 7 | Through the case the company renegotiated **190+ leases** and exited **170+ locations**, cutting annual rent/tenancy expense by **$800M+** and shedding roughly **$12 billion (>50%)** of future lease commitments, with **$400M** new equity capital | **[reported]** — company emergence announcement |
| 8 | WeWork **emerged 2024-06-11** as a private company after plan confirmation | **[reported]** — press; confirmation order **[documented]** on the docket |

**A measurement note the record layer exists for:** the restructuring figures circulate in
three different units — *projected* rent savings (~$8B, April 2024 company projection),
*achieved annual expense* reduction (>$800M/yr), and *total future commitments* shed
(~$12B). They are different measures of different things. Mixing them is exactly the
denominator error the evidence courses warn about; any port of this case must keep the
three separate, as above.

## The reading — decision, not outcome

1. **Lease length is the business, not a term sheet detail
   ([A2](../../curriculum/courses/04-the-asset.md)).** The 2019 S-1 put a ~15-year fixed
   obligation book against revenue the customer could cancel monthly. That is not an
   occupancy problem or a growth problem; it is a **duration mismatch** — the five
   commercial classes are five businesses precisely because lease length sets who bears
   which risk, and this structure took landlord-length obligations against
   hotel-length revenue.
2. **Coverage is the survival test
   ([C1](../../curriculum/courses/05-capital-structure.md), [N3](../../curriculum/courses/03-the-numbers.md)).**
   Fixed obligations that revenue can walk away from fail the coverage question in any
   sustained downturn — the pandemic supplied the downturn, but the structure predates it
   (claim 1 is four years older than claim 3).
3. **The record answered in advance.** Claims 1 and 2 were *published disclosure*,
   available to anyone in August 2019. The platform's whole thesis in one case: reading
   the record beat every narrative, and the certainty error was believing the story over
   the filing.
4. **The landlord's side of §365 ([A4, A6](../../curriculum/courses/04-the-asset.md)).**
   Chapter 11 lets a debtor-tenant reject or renegotiate leases (claims 5–7). A
   single-tenant building underwritten on a long lease to a leveraged tenant carries the
   tenant's balance sheet inside its own WALT — 170+ exited locations became somebody's
   vacancy. Underwrite the tenant's structure, not just the lease term.
5. **The exit was priced by the next owner's capital (claim 6;
   [M3](../../curriculum/courses/08-market-practice.md)).** The equity answer to a
   duration mismatch was conversion — creditors became owners at the value the
   restructured lease book could actually cover.
