# Who administers the federal programs, state by state

*Last reviewed: 2026-09. The v1.2 mapping promised in the
[roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#5-v12--the-resource-graph): the federal programs of
[`federal-programs.md`](federal-programs.md) reach a deal through a **state
administrator**, and the administrator — not the federal statute — sets the scoring, the
calendar and the paperwork. This table names the two administrators that matter most per
state (the LIHTC allocator and the SHPO) plus whether a state historic credit exists to
stack.*

> **Verify before relying — more than usual.** Agencies get renamed and reorganized, state
> credits lapse and revive with budget cycles, and suballocators come and go. Every LIHTC
> row should be confirmed against the state's current QAP (the search pattern is in
> [`state-county-programs.md §5`](state-county-programs.md#5-search-patterns)); every SHPO
> row against the National Park Service's current SHPO directory; every credit against the
> statute. A ✔ means "a program existed as of the review date", never "funded this year".

## How to read the columns

- **LIHTC allocator** — who writes the QAP and awards 9%/4% credits. Usually, but not
  always, the housing finance agency from the
  [50-state table](../states/README.md#the-50-state-quick-reference); the exceptions are
  the trap (California's allocator is the Treasurer's tax credit committee, not CalHFA;
  Georgia's is DCA; Massachusetts' is the housing secretariat, not MassHousing).
- **SHPO** — the office that runs federal historic-credit Part 1/2/3 review and any state
  credit's certification. Names below are the commonly used office name.
- **State historic credit** — ✔ / — as of the review date; the strongest are flagged in
  the [state guides](../states/README.md).

## The other administrators, by pattern

The remaining federal channels route predictably enough that a pattern beats a 51-row
table (search patterns in [`state-county-programs.md §5`](state-county-programs.md#5-search-patterns)):

| Federal program | State-side administrator pattern |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| HOME / CDBG / HTF (non-entitlement areas) | The state's housing or community/economic development department — findable via the state's consolidated plan, same as a county's |
| Section 8 vouchers | PHAs (city/county/regional); HUD's PHA directory lists every one |
| Brownfields §128(a) / VCP | The state environmental agency's voluntary cleanup or land-recycling program |
| C-PACE | A state-designated program administrator or district; the enabling statute names it |
| USDA programs | USDA Rural Development **state offices** — federal staff, state-boundary organized |
| Weatherization / LIHEAP | The state energy or community-services office, delivered through community action agencies |
| National Housing Trust Fund | Almost always the LIHTC allocator above |
| Opportunity Zones | No administrator — tract designations are fixed; the state economic-development office usually keeps the map |

## Why this table earns its place

The LIHTC exceptions row is the whole argument: send a 9% application to CalHFA or
MassHousing and you have mailed it to the wrong agency. The allocator's QAP is the real
zoning of affordable housing ([federal-programs §3](federal-programs.md#3-the-tax-credit-stack));
the SHPO's Part 2 timeline is a schedule gate as real as entitlement
([D3](../../curriculum/courses/06-development-delivery.md)); and whether a state credit
exists to stack decides conversion feasibility before any pro forma opens
([the cross-reference](state-county-programs.md#4-the-cross-reference)).
