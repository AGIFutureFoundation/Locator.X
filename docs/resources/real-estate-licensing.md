# Real-estate professional licensing — an honest scaffold, not yet sourced

*Last reviewed: 2026-10. Zero states sourced. This file states that plainly rather than
leaving the gap implicit, the same discipline [`docs/states/coverage/`](../states/coverage/README.md)
applies to public records: a category this repository has not measured is named as unmeasured,
never left blank and never guessed at.*

> **This is not legal advice and not a substitute for your state's own licensing authority.**
> Pre-licensing education hours, exam content, fees, background-check requirements,
> continuing-education cycles and reciprocity agreements are set by each state's real-estate
> commission (or equivalent agency) and change by statute and rule. Nothing in this file states
> a specific state's current requirement. Verify directly with your state's licensing authority
> before relying on anything here for a filing, an application or an exam date.

## Why this file exists, and why every row says "not yet sourced"

Locator.X's own hard rule — measure before asserting — applies here exactly as it applies to
a parcel's sale price or a use code: **a licensing requirement typed from memory is a
fabricated requirement**, no different in kind from a fabricated coordinate. This container
has no network egress (see the environment note in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)), so none of
the fifty states' current pre-licensing hour counts, exam vendors, fee schedules or renewal
cycles have been pulled, checked or dated. Writing a plausible-looking number into this table
instead of leaving it honestly blank would be worse than having no table at all — it is
exactly the failure mode [`docs/PULL_RECIPE.md`](../PULL_RECIPE.md)'s hard rules exist to
prevent, applied to a legal fact instead of a market one.

## Status vocabulary

Same shape as the record-coverage inventories, applied to a regulatory category instead of a
property-record feed:

| Status | Meaning | What it takes to advance |
|--------|---------|---------------------------|
| `not yet sourced` | No requirement for this state/category has been pulled from the state's own licensing authority | A direct citation (statute, regulation, or the agency's own published candidate handbook), dated, via the desk-browser pull protocol or a platform-owner-supplied source |
| `named` | The licensing authority (agency name, statute citation) is identified but its current requirements have not been transcribed | A pull of the agency's own current candidate handbook or regulation text |
| `sourced YYYY-MM` | A specific requirement is transcribed with its citation and the date it was checked | Nothing — re-verify periodically, since statutes and rules change |

Every row below is `not yet sourced`. There are no `named` or `sourced` rows yet.

## The license categories (general industry structure, not a state-specific claim)

These category names are standard terminology across the US real-estate industry — naming
that a category exists is not a claim about any one state's specific requirements for it:

- **Salesperson / agent license** — the entry-level license; in nearly every state, a
  salesperson must work under a supervising broker and cannot operate independently.
- **Broker license** — a license that, depending on the state, permits operating
  independently and/or supervising salespersons; states commonly (not universally) require
  prior salesperson experience plus additional education before a broker license is issued.
- **Property management license or permit** — the most inconsistent category across states:
  some states require a specific property-management license or endorsement, some fold
  property management under the existing broker license with no separate credential, and
  some impose no state license requirement for property management at all. Which bucket a
  given state falls into is itself a fact this file has not yet sourced.

Beyond these three, some states license or certify additional specialties (auctioneer,
timeshare, business broker) that are out of scope for this first pass.

## What it would take to advance a row

Same mechanics as a county parcel pull, pointed at a different kind of source:

1. **Identify the agency** — the state's real-estate commission or equivalent licensing
   board, and its own published candidate handbook or statute, not a third-party test-prep
   site (those are frequently stale or state the requirements of the course vendor's home
   state by mistake).
2. **Pull the current text**, dated. For a published PDF candidate handbook or statute, this
   is a direct citation rather than a desk-browser pull — the protocol in
   [`docs/PULL_RECIPE.md`](../PULL_RECIPE.md) was built for paginated ArcGIS parcel layers,
   not static regulatory documents, so citing the agency's own published handbook directly
   (with its URL and the date it was retrieved) is the right-sized version of the same
   discipline for this category.
3. **Record the row** here as `sourced YYYY-MM` with the citation, replacing `not yet
   sourced` — never the other direction.
4. **Re-verify periodically.** Unlike a parcel's recorded sale, a licensing requirement
   changes by statute and rule-making, not by a new transaction — a `sourced` row is a
   snapshot, not a permanent fact, and the date is the warning label.
