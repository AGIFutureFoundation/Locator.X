# The two-part ask

Locator.X, Inc. is a separate company. **AGI Corp is its founder** and holds founder
equity; the seed round sells the rest. Alongside it, and legally apart from it, AGI
Property Holdings sponsors a real-estate bundle that runs on the platform.

An investor may take **Part A**, **Part B**, or both. Taking one does not create a right
to the other, neither is conditioned on the other, and each is a separate subscription
with its own documents.

Everything on this page is a **management plan**. It is not an offer, and no figure is a
commitment.

## The firewall, stated before the earmarks

This is the part that matters more than any number on this page.

**Part A conveys rights in Locator.X, Inc. and nothing else.** A Part A investor has no
ownership of, and no claim on, any property, any SPV, or any distribution from the Basket.
Locator.X, Inc. earns platform fees and a documented promote; that is **revenue of a
software company**, an asset on its own balance sheet, and it is not a property interest
however it is described in conversation.

**Part B conveys the economics of the Basket and nothing else.** A Part B investor holds
no equity in Locator.X, Inc., AGI Corp, or any other group entity, and has no claim on
platform revenue.

**No cross-collateralisation.** Basket assets never secure an obligation of Locator.X,
Inc. Locator.X, Inc.'s assets never secure an obligation of the Basket. Separate entities,
separate bank accounts, separate books, separate auditors' letters where applicable.

**The tie between them is contractual and disclosed.** The Basket uses the Locator.X
platform under a written, arm's-length services agreement with a fee schedule fixed in
advance. That agreement is what "tied to the corporate holdings" means here. It does not
mean shared ownership, and no material may say or imply that it does.

**The conflict this creates is named, not buried.** The platform that recommends an
acquisition also earns a fee when the Basket completes it. That is a real conflict and it
is managed rather than denied — see the controls in
[`PORTFOLIO_BASKET.md`](PORTFOLIO_BASKET.md) and risk 25 in
[`RISK_REGISTER.md`](RISK_REGISTER.md).

## Part A — Locator.X, Inc. seed: where the $4.0M goes

Earmarks are **management targets**, and they are not invented for this page: each maps to
a gap the repository already measures and publishes. The percentages bracket 100%, which
`scripts/validate_company.py` verifies arithmetically.

| # | Earmark | Share | What it buys, and the measured gap it closes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Record-layer expansion** | 26%–34% | County and GIS record sessions to advance coverage rows from *named* and *pulled* to *shipped*. Today 8 of 90 rows are shipped — this is the single largest gap on the board, and depth of record is the product |
| 2 | **Collaboration layer** | 14%–20% | Multi-user state, task and approval workflow, audit trail across users. One build that unblocks three partial agents *and* investment-committee approval, which is acquisition-gate condition 8 |
| 3 | **The two unbuilt products** | 12%–18% | Portfolio Pulse (actuals scored against the original underwrite) and the Risk Ledger. Both are named, specified and absent; Portfolio Pulse is the only feature in the plan that compounds |
| 4 | **Data licences and source agreements** | 8%–12% | Licensed rent and transaction data where the public record does not reach, each with a published error measure rather than an unlabelled estimate |
| 5 | **Security, accessibility, privacy, compliance** | 6%–10% | SOC-readiness, penetration testing, WCAG 2.2 AA, data-processing agreements. Foundational, and the first line an undisciplined budget cuts |
| 6 | **Go-to-market** | 8%–12% | Design partners, paid pilots, implementation. Revenue that reduces the size of the next round |
| 7 | **G&A, legal and reserve** | 8%–12% | Entity formation, the governing documents that do not yet exist, audit readiness, and runway reserve |

**What Part A does not fund:** any property, any renovation, any acquisition deposit, any
reserve for an asset. Operating capital never buys buildings. If the Basket needs money,
the Basket raises it.

## Part B — the bundle: where the $6.0M goes

Detail, controls and the acquisition gate are in
[`PORTFOLIO_BASKET.md`](PORTFOLIO_BASKET.md). The earmarks:

| # | Earmark | Share | Note |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Acquisition equity** | 58%–66% | The equity portion of purchase price across the basket's defined assets |
| 2 | **Renovation and capex** | 15%–21% | Fixed approved scope plus contingency, with change-order controls and dual approval |
| 3 | **Closing, legal, appraisal, inspection** | 4%–7% | Independent diligence on every asset — never the sponsor's own opinion of value |
| 4 | **Reserves** | 8%–12% | Debt service, insurance, operating and capital reserves, funded at close rather than hoped for |
| 5 | **Formation and basket administration** | 2%–4% | Entity formation, operating agreement, subscription documents, fund administration |
| 6 | **Contingency** | 1%–3% | Unallocated, and released only by the stated approval authority |

**What Part B does not fund:** platform engineering, product development, sales or any
operating expense of Locator.X, Inc. Property capital enters a named vehicle for named
assets. The services agreement is a disclosed fee, paid at a schedule fixed in advance,
not a route for the Basket to subsidise the software company.

## Why the two parts are offered together, and why they stay apart

The reason to run them side by side is real: a platform that underwrites real estate is
worth more when its own operator is using it on live assets, and a basket underwritten on
an evidence-graded platform is diligenced better than one underwritten in a spreadsheet.
Each makes the other better.

The reason to keep them legally apart is equally real, and it is not merely compliance.
A software investor placed into property risk they did not choose, or a property investor
discovering their capital funded an engineering team, is a fact pattern that ends in
litigation — and it usually starts with a deck that put both on one slide and called it
one opportunity.

**One cheque does not buy both.** Two cheques, two sets of documents, two sets of
economics, and an investor who can state which one they hold.

## Before either part moves

Every instrument, subscription document, operating agreement, services agreement and
investor communication requires review and approval by qualified securities, tax,
real-estate and fund counsel before external use ([`FUNDING.md`](FUNDING.md)). This
repository does not issue, market or solicit anything, and the readiness checklist in
[`SAFE_TERMS.md`](SAFE_TERMS.md) is mostly unbuilt.
