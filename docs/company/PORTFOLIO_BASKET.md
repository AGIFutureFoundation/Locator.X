# Locator.X Portfolio Basket I — the real-estate bundle

The bundle is a defined basket of real-estate assets, sponsored by AGI Property Holdings,
sourced and underwritten on the Locator.X platform, and held in a vehicle that is
**legally separate from the software company**. It is Part B of
[`THE_ASK.md`](THE_ASK.md).

Everything here is a **management plan**. No asset has been acquired, no vehicle has been
formed, and nothing on this page is an offer.

## What "tied to the corporate holdings" means, precisely

It means three specific things, all contractual and all disclosed:

1. **AGI Property Holdings, LLC sponsors the Basket.** Sponsorship is a role — forming the
   vehicle, assembling the assets, and standing behind the operating agreement — not a
   guarantee of outcome.
2. **The Basket runs on the Locator.X platform** under a written, arm's-length services
   agreement with a fee schedule fixed in advance: evidence-graded sourcing, the
   underwriting desk, the strategy switchboard, the coverage panel, and the record locker.
3. **AGI Development Management, LLC operates the assets** under a separate written
   agreement.

It does **not** mean shared ownership. A holder of Basket interests owns Basket economics.
A holder of Locator.X, Inc. equity owns a software company. The firewall is in
[`THE_ASK.md`](THE_ASK.md) and it is not softened anywhere.

## The basket thesis

The platform's advantage is depth of record, not breadth of contact. The Basket is
therefore built where the record is deepest — **inside the counties Locator.X has actually
shipped**, not across a national map. Where the coverage inventory says a county publishes
no dated sale price, the Basket does not buy there on a valuation the platform itself
grades as unsupported.

That constraint is the whole point. A sponsor whose own system says *we cannot value this
here* and buys anyway has learned nothing from building the system.

## The conflict, named and controlled

**The platform that recommends an acquisition earns a fee when the Basket completes it.**
That is a genuine conflict of interest. It is disclosed here rather than discovered later,
and it is controlled:

| Control | What it prevents |
|---|---|
| Fee schedule fixed in advance, in writing, disclosed to every Basket investor | A fee that grows with the deal the sponsor chose |
| **No platform fee on a deal the investment committee rejects** | An incentive to push volume through the committee |
| Independent third-party appraisal and inspection on every asset | The sponsor's own model standing in for a valuation |
| Investment committee with at least one member independent of Locator.X, Inc. | A committee that is the recommender in another chair |
| The acquisition gate in [`PORTFOLIO_STRATEGY.md`](PORTFOLIO_STRATEGY.md), all eight conditions, minuted | A gate applied loosely under deal pressure |
| Coverage panel output attached to every investment memo | An asset bought on a valuation the platform grades as unsupported |
| Related-party disclosure in every quarterly report, whether or not anything occurred | A disclosure section that appears only when it is empty |

**Two of the eight acquisition-gate conditions have no platform support at all** —
concentration limits and investment-committee approval — and are run manually until the
collaboration layer in Part A is built. A gate believed to be automated and is not is worse
than a gate everyone knows is manual, so it is stated here and on the gate itself.

## What the platform actually contributes to an acquisition

Measured, not asserted. Each of these ships today and is covered by a CI gate:

- **Evidence grading per source**, so a memo states the quality of its own inputs
- **A comparable is a recorded sale with a date**, or it is not a comparable — and an
  assessed value is never presented as a transaction
- **The strategy switchboard**, comparing hold, flip, BRRRR, house-hack and conversion on
  one record, each graded by the facts it rests on and refusing to rank columns resting on
  different quality of evidence
- **The coverage panel**, stating what the edition cannot answer at all
- **The underwriting desk**, with ten financing structures and a stress block: break-even
  rent, break-even rate, rate headroom, DSCR under stress

What it does **not** contribute, and what must therefore be bought from outside on every
asset: title, survey, physical inspection, environmental, insurance quotes, and a lender's
own view. The platform narrows the field and prices the question. It does not close a deal.

## Reporting to Basket investors

Quarterly, per [`INVESTOR_REPORTING.md`](INVESTOR_REPORTING.md), and specifically:

- asset-by-asset performance against the **original underwrite**, not against a revised one
- occupancy, rent achieved, capex spent against budget, days vacant
- debt service coverage and reserve balances
- every related-party payment made to a Locator.X or AGI entity in the period
- material incidents, and what was done about them

The first item is the one that matters and the one most sponsors quietly drop. An
underwrite that is revised and then reported against cannot be wrong, which is precisely
why it is worthless. **Actuals are scored against what was underwritten at acquisition**,
and the variance is published to investors whichever direction it runs.

That discipline is also the data that makes Portfolio Pulse real — the product named in
Part A earmark 3 — which is the honest version of the link between the two parts: **the
Basket produces the evidence, and the platform learns from it.** Not shared ownership. A
feedback loop, documented, at arm's length.

## What is not claimed

No projected return, no target yield, no distribution promise, no assurance that any asset
will be acquired or that the Basket will be formed. Property values, rents, rates,
liquidity, insurance cost, construction cost and regulation all move
([`RISK_REGISTER.md`](RISK_REGISTER.md) rows 15–17). Real-estate investments are illiquid
and may result in the loss of the entire investment.
